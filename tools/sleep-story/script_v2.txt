T: Who Really Spoke to Pharaoh?

You have probably heard that Moses went to speak to Pharaoh.
That is the story most of us heard our whole lives.
But what does the Bible really say about it?

If this helps you rest, tap like and subscribe, so other tired people can find it.
Now lie back, and let the day go quiet.

Tonight's story is about a man who did not want the job.

Moses had been raised in Pharaoh's house, but by now he was a shepherd in the desert, far from Egypt.
Then God spoke to him from a bush that burned and did not burn up.
In Exodus chapter three, verse ten, God said:
» Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt.

Moses did not jump at it.
In verse eleven he said:
» Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh, and that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?
Most of us would have said the same thing.
? What has God asked of you that you keep answering with, who am I?

Then he gave God a list of reasons.
He worried the people would not believe him.
And then he came to the real trouble, which was his mouth.
In Exodus chapter four, verse ten, Moses said:
» O my LORD, I am not eloquent, neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken unto thy servant:
» but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.
? What is the one thing about yourself you were sure God could never use?

Listen to how God answers him.
He does not tell Moses he is wrong about himself.
He asks a question.
» Who hath made man's mouth? or who maketh the dumb, or deaf, or the seeing, or the blind? have not I the LORD?
Then He tells him what to do.
» Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.
? If God made the very thing you are ashamed of, how does He see it?

That should have settled it, but Moses asked one more time.
» O my LORD, send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send.
In plain words, he was asking God to send somebody else.

And here is the part we tend to skip over.
The Bible says the anger of the Lord was kindled against Moses.
Even then, God did not take the job away from him.
He answered with help.
» Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother? I know that he can speak well.
» And also, behold, he cometh forth to meet thee: and when he seeth thee, he will be glad in his heart.
Aaron was already on his way, and he was glad to see his brother.

Then God laid out how it would work, and it is worth going slowly here.
» And thou shalt speak unto him, and put words in his mouth: and I will be with thy mouth, and with his mouth, and will teach you what ye shall do.
» And he shall be thy spokesman unto the people:
God gave the words to Moses. Moses gave them to Aaron. Aaron spoke them to the people.
? Who has God put beside you for the part you cannot do alone?

So Moses went back to Egypt, and the two brothers gathered the elders of Israel.
Exodus chapter four, verses thirty and thirty-one, tell us what happened.
» And Aaron spake all the words which the LORD had spoken unto Moses, and did the signs in the sight of the people.
» And the people believed: and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had looked upon their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshipped.

Let your shoulders go loose now. There is no hurry in this story.

When it came time to face Pharaoh himself, God said the same thing again.
In Exodus chapter seven, verses one and two:
» See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.
» Thou shalt speak all that I command thee: and Aaron thy brother shall speak unto Pharaoh, that he send the children of Israel out of his land.
Verse seven gives us their ages.
» And Moses was fourscore years old, and Aaron fourscore and three years old, when they spake unto Pharaoh.
Two old men, eighty and eighty-three, walking into the palace of Egypt's king.
Verse ten tells us what Aaron did with his rod.
» and Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh, and before his servants, and it became a serpent.

So who really spoke to Pharaoh?
A good deal of the speaking fell to Aaron, with Moses right beside him, and God behind them both.
God did not wait for Moses to feel ready.
He sent help to stand beside the weak spot, and He worked through both men.

Now look at what Aaron became.
Hebrews chapter five, verse four, says:
» And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron.
Aaron became the high priest, the man who stood between God and the people.
That points to Someone greater.
Hebrews chapter four, verses fourteen and fifteen:
» Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God,
» For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.
Moses told God about his weakness, and God sent a brother.
Our weakness is known too, by a high priest who has been through all of it.

And He is not far off.
Hebrews chapter seven, verse twenty-five:
» Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.
First John chapter two, verse one:
» And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:

So here is one thing to do with tonight.
Tell God plainly where you feel slow, or small, or short of words, the way Moses did.
He heard Moses out, and He will hear you out.
Hebrews chapter four, verse sixteen:
» Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

The same God who sent a brother to a man slow of speech has sent His own Son to stand for you.
You do not need to find the right words tonight.
He is already speaking for you.
Let that be the last thing you hold as you drift off.
Rest now. You are in good hands.
