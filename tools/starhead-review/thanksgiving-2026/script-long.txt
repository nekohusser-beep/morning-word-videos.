When's the last time you said thank you and really meant it? I don't mean the one you toss at the cashier on your way out the door. I mean the kind where it hits you that somebody gave you something you did not earn.

Most of us do our biggest thanking of the year about four seconds before dinner today. Somebody clears their throat, everybody's looking at the gravy, and the amen comes out like a starting gun. Ten lepers got healed one day, and only one came back to say thank you.

Today I want to give that word a few minutes. Thanks. The Bible has a lot more to say about it than we usually do, and I think it might change how you sit down at that table.

Open your Bible with me to Psalm 107, verse 1.

[[O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.]]

The psalm gives two reasons. He is good, and His mercy does not run out. It doesn't wait for a good year before it asks for thanks. It hangs the whole thing on who He is, and He was the same on your worst day.

Think about the last time you were truly thankful. Maybe somebody covered a bill you couldn't, or a friend drove two hours when they didn't have to. What was going on inside you? You felt small and glad at the same time. Small, because you knew you hadn't earned it. Glad, because it came anyway.

That's what thankfulness is. Humility and gladness in one heart. A proud man can be happy about what he gets, but he can't be thankful, because he figures he had it coming. You have to know you got more than you deserved before the word means anything. And that's the exact word the psalm uses. Mercy. Getting what you didn't earn.

Jesus saw this play out one day, and Luke wrote it down. Luke chapter 17. He's heading into a village and ten men with leprosy meet Him. They stand way off, because the law kept them away from everybody. Family, work, worship. And they lift their voices and ask for one thing.

[[Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.]]

Have mercy. Same word as the psalm. He tells them to go show themselves to the priests. Nobody gets healed first. They turn and start walking on His word, and somewhere on the road it happens. They're cleansed. All ten.

Now Luke tells us what came next, and I'll read it straight.

[[And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan.]]

One out of ten. And Jesus noticed.

[[Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?]]

The Bible doesn't tell us where the nine went. I'll tell you what I think, and I'm guessing here. I don't think they were bad men. They'd been cut off for years. They had families to find, a priest to see, a whole life to catch up on. They were busy. All of them did what Jesus said and kept walking.

That's why this one gets me. Most days I'd be one of the nine, and I bet you would too. Nobody decides to be ungrateful. We get busy, and by Thursday the gift is just how things are.

Jesus had one more thing to say, and it went to the man who came back.

[[Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.]]

Ten got healed. One got to hear Jesus say he was made whole. The nine walked off with the gift. He walked off with the Giver.

David knew we'd forget. Listen to what he told his own soul in Psalm 103, verse 2.

[[Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits:]]

Forget not. A king had to say that to himself. So we can quit acting like thankfulness comes naturally.

What's on your table today? The food, sure. The people around it. The job that paid for the food, the truck that got you there. James tells us where all of it comes from. James chapter 1, verse 17.

[[Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.]]

Everything good comes down. Thanksgiving is how it goes back up. Somebody gave you something, and you turn around and say so. That finishes the circle. Leave it off and the gift just sits in your hand while you start to think you built it yourself.

Paul traced a lot of what goes wrong with people back to this spot. Romans chapter 1, verse 21.

[[Because that, when they knew God, they glorified him not as God, neither were thankful; but became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish heart was darkened.]]

Neither were thankful. The slide started when they quit saying thanks. The big wicked stuff came after.

Now thankfulness does something for you too, and I'm glad it does. Paul wrote about worry in Philippians chapter 4, verses 6 and 7.

[[Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.]]

See where the thanks sits? Right in the middle of the asking. Bring the request, and bring the thank you with it, in the same prayer. Then the peace of God stands guard over your heart and your mind.

People who study this keep finding the same thing. Folks who make a habit of giving thanks tend to sleep better. They worry less. Their friendships last. They bounce back quicker when life knocks them down, and they get more generous. The researchers are catching up to something Paul wrote down about two thousand years ago. Thanks doesn't replace a doctor, and nobody should tell you it does. God did build us so a thankful heart runs better than a bitter one, though.

Then there's the verse people throw at you when you're having a bad week. First Thessalonians chapter 5, verse 18.

[[In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.]]

Look at the little word in there. In. It says in every thing. It doesn't say for every thing. You don't have to call a bad day good. You can look around in the middle of it and find what He still gave you. A paycheck that came in short, but it came. A person who picked up the phone.

If there's an empty chair at your table this year, you don't have to fake anything. Thank God for the time you had with them. That's allowed. The ache and the thanks can sit at the same table.

And if you worked last night so everybody else could have today off, the plant crew, the nurse, the gas station, the guy driving a truck at three in the morning, this is for you too. Somebody is eating a hot meal today because you were on that clock. Thank you. God saw it.

So what would I ask of you today? Don't be one of the nine. Two things.

First, when the table prayer comes, name something out loud, and make it specific. Skip family and food. Say the truck started this morning. Say the lights are on. Specific thanks is how you find out how much you've been handed.

Second, find one person and tell them the actual thing they did for you. Something like, you drove me to that appointment when you had your own mess going on. Watch what it does to their face.

Forgive us for the times we took the gift and kept walking like the nine. Teach us to turn around and say it to You. Thank You for the food on our tables, for the people sitting around them, and for the work that put it there. For the ones working today so the rest of us could rest, we ask You to bless them. Where there's an empty chair, thank You for the time we had. Put a thankful spirit in every home, and let it last well past this holiday. In Jesus' name, amen.
